# References: Ecosystem Ecology, Biogeochemical Cycles, and Conservation

1. [Ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem) - Wikipedia - Overview of energy flow and matter cycling as the two organizing processes of ecosystem ecology developed throughout this chapter.

2. [Biogeochemical cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeochemical_cycle) - Wikipedia - Explains how carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, and water move between living organisms and the abiotic environment.

3. [Nitrogen cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_cycle) - Wikipedia - Detailed description of nitrogen fixation, nitrification, assimilation, and denitrification, the processes central to this chapter's nitrogen cycle section.

4. Fundamentals of Ecology (5th ed.) - Eugene P. Odum, Gary W. Barrett - Cengage - Odum is credited with inventing ecosystem ecology as a teaching discipline itself, pioneering the energy-flow diagram with explicit heat-loss arrows at each trophic level that remains the standard visualization for the ten percent rule.

5. Essentials of Conservation Biology (6th ed.) - Richard B. Primack - Sinauer - Primack pioneered the case-study-driven structure of conservation biology textbooks, pairing each core concept — habitat fragmentation, minimum viable population, invasive species — with a real, ongoing conservation program rather than a purely theoretical treatment.

6. [AP Biology: Ecology](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/ap-biology/ecology) - Khan Academy - Free video lessons and practice problems on energy flow, biogeochemical cycles, and conservation biology aligned to the AP Biology course framework.

7. [HHMI BioInteractive](https://www.biointeractive.org/) - Howard Hughes Medical Institute - Short films and interactive modules on ecosystem ecology and conservation, including data-based activities on climate change and biodiversity loss.

8. [Biology 2e](https://openstax.org/details/books/biology-2e) - OpenStax - Free college biology textbook chapter on ecosystems with additional worked energy-pyramid and biogeochemical cycle diagrams.

9. [Understanding Science](https://undsci.berkeley.edu/) - UC Berkeley Museum of Paleontology - Teaching resource on how ecologists gather and interpret evidence for global change, relevant to the climate change section of this chapter.

10. [7.013 Introductory Biology](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/7-013-introductory-biology-spring-2018/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - College-level lecture notes covering ecosystem ecology and the biology of global environmental change.
