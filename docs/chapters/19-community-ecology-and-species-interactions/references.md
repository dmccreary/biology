# References: Community Ecology and Species Interactions

1. [Community (ecology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_(ecology)) - Wikipedia - Overview of interspecific interactions and the community-level structure this chapter examines, from predation to mutualism.

2. [Keystone species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_species) - Wikipedia - Explains the disproportionate community-wide effect of species like sea otters, including the concept's origin in Robert Paine's field experiments.

3. [Ecological succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_succession) - Wikipedia - Describes primary and secondary succession and the climax community concept covered in this chapter's closing sections.

4. Ecology (6th ed.) - Charles J. Krebs - Pearson - Krebs is credited with pioneering the explicit use of classic manipulative field experiments — Paine's sea star removals, Connell's barnacle zonation study — as the primary teaching vehicle for interspecific interaction concepts, rather than describing competition and predation abstractly.

5. The Theory of Island Biogeography - Robert H. MacArthur, Edward O. Wilson - Princeton University Press - MacArthur and Wilson originated the immigration-extinction equilibrium graph, now the universal teaching diagram for explaining species richness on islands and habitat fragments in every ecology textbook.

6. [AP Biology: Ecology](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/ap-biology/ecology) - Khan Academy - Free video lessons and practice problems on community ecology, species interactions, and succession aligned to the AP Biology course framework.

7. [HHMI BioInteractive](https://www.biointeractive.org/) - Howard Hughes Medical Institute - Short films and interactive modules on species interactions and trophic cascades, including the Yellowstone wolf reintroduction case study.

8. [Biology 2e](https://openstax.org/details/books/biology-2e) - OpenStax - Free college biology textbook chapter on community ecology with additional worked examples of resource partitioning and niche overlap.

9. [Understanding Evolution](https://evolution.berkeley.edu/) - UC Museum of Paleontology, UC Berkeley - Teaching resource covering coevolution and convergent evolution, relevant to the predator-prey and pollinator relationships in this chapter.

10. [7.013 Introductory Biology](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/7-013-introductory-biology-spring-2018/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - College-level lecture notes covering community ecology and species interaction models.
