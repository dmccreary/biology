# References: The Central Dogma: DNA Replication and Protein Synthesis

1. [DNA replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_replication) - Wikipedia - Detailed description of the semiconservative mechanism, the enzymes at the replication fork, and the leading/lagging strand problem covered in this chapter.

2. [Transcription (biology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_(biology)) - Wikipedia - Explains how RNA polymerase reads a DNA template and how eukaryotic mRNA is processed with a 5' cap, poly-A tail, and splicing.

3. [Genetic code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_code) - Wikipedia - Describes the 64-codon table, its redundancy and universality, and how codons are translated into the 20 standard amino acids.

4. Molecular Biology of the Gene (8th ed.) - James D. Watson, Tania A. Baker, Stephen P. Bell, Alexander Gann, Michael Levine, Richard Losick - Pearson - Watson's original text is credited with originating the now-standard practice of teaching replication, transcription, and translation as one continuous narrative of information flow rather than as three isolated topics.

5. Molecular Biology of the Cell (7th ed.) - Bruce Alberts, Alexander Johnson, Julian Lewis, David Morgan, Martin Raff, Keith Roberts, Peter Walter - W.W. Norton - Alberts pioneered the color-coded leading/lagging strand diagram with labeled Okazaki fragments that remains the standard way replication fork mechanics are visualized in introductory textbooks.

6. [AP Biology: Gene Expression and Regulation](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/ap-biology/gene-expression-and-regulation) - Khan Academy - Free video lessons and practice problems on DNA replication, transcription, and translation aligned to the AP Biology course framework.

7. [Learn.Genetics](https://learn.genetics.utah.edu/) - University of Utah - Interactive animations of DNA replication, transcription, and translation designed to make the molecular machinery of gene expression visible step by step.

8. [Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21054/) - National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI Bookshelf) - Free full-text chapters on DNA replication and RNA synthesis, covering the enzymology of the replication fork in molecular detail.

9. [HHMI BioInteractive](https://www.biointeractive.org/) - Howard Hughes Medical Institute - Short films and interactive modules on DNA structure, replication, and protein synthesis, including animations of the ribosome in action.

10. [Biology 2e](https://openstax.org/details/books/biology-2e) - OpenStax - Free college biology textbook chapter on DNA replication and protein synthesis with additional worked codon-translation examples.
