# Image Generation Prompt — Chloroplast & Light Reactions

## File
`chloroplast.png` — 1200×900 px, landscape 4:3

## Prompt

A detailed biological textbook illustration of a chloroplast cross-section showing both
the overall organelle structure and a zoomed cross-section of the thylakoid membrane
with embedded protein complexes of the light reactions. Clean line art with light color
fills, white background.

**Overall chloroplast structure (upper two-thirds of the image):**

Show the chloroplast as a cut-away ellipse revealing its internal structure:
- Outer membrane — smooth pale green outer boundary curve
- Inner membrane — a second boundary curve just inside the outer membrane, slightly
  darker green; a visible gap (intermembrane space) separates the two layers
- Stroma — large pale green aqueous interior between the inner membrane and the thylakoids;
  clearly the dominant space of the organelle
- Two to three grana — stacks of 5–7 tightly stacked disc-shaped thylakoids, rendered
  as dark green layered discs; one granum centered in the image, one or two at slight
  angles in the background; the discs should be visually distinct like a stack of coins
- Stroma lamellae — thin membrane tubes connecting adjacent grana stacks, visible as
  thin green ribbons
- Thylakoid lumen — visible as a slightly lighter interior color within each thylakoid
  disc when shown in cross-section; must be visually distinguishable from the membrane
  and from the stroma

**Thylakoid membrane zoom (lower third of the image):**

A close-up cross-section of a single thylakoid membrane showing both the stromal side
(top of the zoom) and the lumenal side (bottom of the zoom). The membrane itself is
rendered as a bilayer strip running horizontally. Protein complexes are embedded in the
membrane from left to right in order of electron flow:

1. **PSII (Photosystem II)** — large blue multi-subunit complex; on its lumenal face
   show a small water molecule (H₂O) and an O₂ bubble, indicating water splitting
2. **Cytochrome b6f complex** — large purple transmembrane complex in the center
3. **PSI (Photosystem I)** — large orange-red multi-subunit complex, right of center
4. **ATP synthase (CF₁)** — rightmost complex; a large pink mushroom-shaped protein with
   a cylindrical CF₀ domain embedded in the membrane and a globular CF₁ catalytic head
   protruding upward into the stroma

Between complexes show:
- **Plastoquinone (PQ)** — a small orange oval within the lipid bilayer between PSII
  and Cyt b6f; show a curved arrow indicating its lateral movement
- **Plastocyanin (PC)** — a small teal oval in the lumenal space between Cyt b6f and PSI

Above the membrane (in the stroma) show:
- **Ferredoxin (Fd)** — a small reddish oval above PSI on the stromal face
- **NADP⁺ / NADPH** — yellow coenzyme symbol above and right of PSI, with a curved
  arrow showing reduction (NADP⁺ → NADPH)

Below the ATP synthase show:
- **Proton gradient arrow** — a thick orange downward-curved arrow from the lumen
  through the ATP synthase stalk into the stroma, labeled with H⁺, indicating
  proton flow direction

**No text of any kind in the image.** No labels, no chemical formula text, no arrows
with text, no callout lines, no annotation marks, no legend, no title, no figure caption
anywhere in the image.

Each structure must use a **distinct color** that matches the color descriptions above
so that callout markers can be visually matched to structures during calibration.

The outer membrane, inner membrane, stroma, grana, and thylakoid lumen (upper section)
should be clearly visible with enough open space between structures so that circular
marker dots (≈3–5% of image width) can be placed without overlapping adjacent structures.

Style: biological textbook illustration, clean line art with flat color fills,
white background. Suitable for college placement Biology high school level.
Aspect ratio: landscape 4:3. Resolution: 1200×900 px.
