    Image Generation Prompt — DNA Double Helix Structure

    Technical

    File: dna-double-helix.png Size: 800 px wide × 1200 px height, portrait orientation Style: Scientific textbook illustration, clean line art with rich flat color fills, white background, no gradients.

    A DNA double helix viewed from the side, helix axis running vertically (top to bottom), completing approximately 3 full helical turns visible in the image.

    Critical constraint

    Absolutely no text, labels, arrows, leader lines, callout numbers, annotation marks, or any typography anywhere in the image. All labeling is handled externally.

    Subject

    A right-handed B-form DNA double helix shown from the side. The helix axis runs vertically, top to bottom. The molecule shows approximately 3 full helical turns, containing roughly 9–10 visible base pairs per turn. Both the 5′ end (top) and 3′ end (bottom) of each strand are clearly visible at the image edges.

    The Two Backbone Strands

    Draw the sugar-phosphate backbone as two continuous thick ribbon-like strands:

    • Left strand: deep cornflower blue (#4A90D9), sinuous ribbon ~25 px wide
    • Right strand: warm crimson red (#C0392B), sinuous ribbon ~25 px wide, running antiparallel

    Both ribbons spiral around the central vertical axis. At the "crossover" points (top and bottom of each turn) the strands pass behind each other, giving depth cues. The strands occupy the outer ~15–30% of image width on each side.

    Deoxyribose Sugars

    Each sugar is a regular pentagon (5-sided polygon, ~30 px across) attached directly to the backbone ribbon, pointing inward toward the central axis. Color: light tan / burlywood (#DEB887). Show approximately 9–10 sugars per strand, evenly spaced along each backbone.

    Phosphate Groups

    Between each adjacent pair of deoxyribose sugar pentagons on each backbone, show a rounded circular node (~18 px diameter) in bright lime-green (#7DB84A). These phosphate groups sit on the backbone ribbon between the sugars.

    Base Pairs (the horizontal rungs)

    The bases form horizontal rungs connecting the two strands, like a twisted ladder. Show all four base types with distinct colors and sizes:

    • Adenine (A): medium teal (#1ABC9C) — wider purine; draw as a wider flat rectangle extending from the left backbone inward
    • Thymine (T): bright yellow (#F1C40F) — narrower pyrimidine; extends from the right backbone inward; always shown paired with Adenine
    • Guanine (G): medium purple (#8E44AD) — wider purine; extends from one backbone inward
    • Cytosine (C): burnt orange (#E67E22) — narrower pyrimidine; always shown paired with Guanine

    Show at least 8 complete base pairs. Use this approximate order top to bottom: A-T, G-C, A-T, C-G, T-A, G-C, A-T, C-G. In A-T pairs: teal (A) on the left backbone side, amber (T) on the right. In G-C pairs: purple (G) on the left backbone side, orange (C) on the right. (Reverse pairing for C-G and T-A pairs.)

    Hydrogen Bonds

    Between the paired bases in each rung, show 2–3 short dashed lines in light gray (#BBBBBB). A-T pairs have 2 dashes; G-C pairs have 3 dashes. Make the dashes clearly visible in the central gap between the two base halves.

    Major and Minor Grooves

    The two grooves alternate along the helix:

    • Major groove: the wider gap between the backbone strands (~60% of image width between strands at its widest angle). Fill the open space in the major groove with a very pale sky-blue wash (#EBF5FB) so it is distinguishable from the background.
    • Minor groove: the narrower gap (~30% of image width). Fill with a very pale warm cream (#FEF9EF).

    The alternating wide/narrow gaps must be clearly distinguishable in the image.

    Polarity End-Caps (5′ and 3′)

    At the very top of the image: - Left strand top end: a small bright cobalt-blue cap (#2471A3) — this is the 5′ end of the left strand - Right strand top end: a small bright crimson cap (#C0392B) — this is the 3′ end of the right strand

    At the very bottom of the image: - Left strand bottom end: a small dark red cap (#922B21) — the 3′ end of the left strand - Right strand bottom end: a small dark navy cap (#1A5276) — the 5′ end of the right strand

    Make the end-caps visually distinct from the main backbone color. A slightly different shade, slightly thicker terminus, or a small capped terminus shape is sufficient.

    Spatial layout and landing zones

    • Leave ~20 px of clear space around each phosphate node and sugar pentagon so callout markers can be placed without overlap.
    • Each of the four base types (A, T, G, C) should appear at least twice so multiple callout options are available.
    • The major and minor grooves should be wide enough to place a callout marker inside without touching the backbone ribbon.
    • The end-cap termini at top and bottom should be separated from the nearest backbone crossover by at least 30 px.

    Do NOT include

    • Any text, numbers, prime marks (5′, 3′), Greek letters, or symbols
    • Figure captions or titles
    • Arrows or leader lines of any kind
    • Histone proteins, nucleosomes, or chromatin
    • Water molecules, ions, or solvent
    • Double-stranded DNA with one strand opened or unwound
