    Protein Structure Levels — Image Prompt

    Purpose

    Generate a single wide illustration showing the four levels of protein structure side by side in four equal panels. The image will be used as a background for an interactive MicroSim where students hover over each panel to learn about that structural level. No text, labels, numbers, arrows, callout lines, leader lines, or annotation marks anywhere in the image.

    Global Specifications

    • Aspect ratio: 4:3 landscape (1600 × 1200 px)
    • Style: Clean biological textbook illustration, precise molecular-level artwork with flat color fills, white background, AP Biology high school level
    • Layout: Four equal-width vertical panels arranged horizontally, separated by thin light-gray divider lines. Each panel contains one level of protein structure.
    • Background: Pure white within each panel
    • No text of any kind in the image. No labels, no level names, no chemical formulas, no numbers, no arrows, no annotation marks. Repeat: absolutely no text.

    Color Palette (consistent across all panels)

    Element Color Hex
    Nonpolar amino acids Warm gray #95A5A6
    Polar amino acids Sky blue #5DADE2
    Positively charged amino acids Coral red #E74C3C
    Negatively charged amino acids Orange #E67E22
    Peptide bond backbone Dark gray #2C3E50
    Hydrogen bonds (dashed) Green #27AE60
    Disulfide bridges Gold/yellow #F1C40F
    Alpha helices Orange-red #E67E22
    Beta sheets Blue #3498DB
    Loops/coils Light gray #BDC3C7
    Heme groups Dark red/maroon #922B21

    Panel 1 — Primary Structure (leftmost)

    Draw a linear chain of amino acids running horizontally across the panel, slightly curved in a gentle S-shape to fill the vertical space.

    • Chain: 10-12 amino acid residues shown as large, distinct colored circles (diameter ~60-70px) connected by thick dark gray lines representing peptide bonds
    • Amino acid colors: Alternate between nonpolar (warm gray), polar (sky blue), positively charged (coral red), and negatively charged (orange) — use a mix showing that the sequence contains different types
    • Circle appearance: Each amino acid is a solid filled circle with a slightly darker outline of the same hue. No letters or abbreviations inside the circles.
    • Peptide bond connections: Thick dark gray lines (~4px) connecting each circle to the next in sequence. The bonds should be clearly visible between the circles.
    • Chain path: The chain should flow from upper-left to lower-right in a gentle, readable curve — NOT perfectly straight, but with a natural slight wave. This shows it is a linear sequence.
    • N-terminus: The leftmost/top circle should have a small triangular arrow pointing inward (indicating the start of the chain) — just a shape, no text
    • C-terminus: The rightmost/bottom circle should have a small square cap (indicating the end) — just a shape, no text
    • Side chains: Small stubs or bumps projecting outward from each circle in different directions, colored to match their parent amino acid. These represent R-groups but should be simple protrusions, not detailed chemical structures.
    • Visual feel: Clean, colorful beads on a string. The emphasis is on LINEAR SEQUENCE — one amino acid after another.

    Panel 2 — Secondary Structure

    Draw two secondary structures side by side within the panel: an alpha helix on the left half and a beta pleated sheet on the right half.

    Alpha Helix (left half of panel):

    • Shape: A right-handed coiled ribbon/helix running vertically, about 5-6 turns visible
    • Ribbon color: Orange-red (#E67E22), with subtle shading to show the 3D coil — the front of the ribbon is brighter, the back is slightly darker
    • Ribbon width: Thick enough to clearly see the helical coil pattern (~30px wide ribbon)
    • Hydrogen bonds: Green dashed lines running vertically along the interior of the helix, connecting one turn to another (every 3.6 residues). Show 4-5 green dashed lines visible between coil turns. These should be clearly visible as dashed green lines, not solid.
    • R-group stubs: Small colored bumps (mix of gray, blue, red) projecting outward from the helix at regular intervals, radiating away from the central axis. About 8-10 stubs visible.
    • Backbone: The orange-red ribbon IS the backbone — no separate backbone line needed

    Beta Pleated Sheet (right half of panel):

    • Shape: 3-4 parallel flat arrows/ribbons running vertically, with a zigzag pleated appearance when viewed from the side
    • Arrow direction: Alternate arrows pointing up and down (antiparallel arrangement). Each arrow/ribbon is blue (#3498DB).
    • Pleating: The ribbons should have a visible zigzag or accordion-fold appearance — they are NOT flat. Show the pleating as gentle up-down waves along each strand.
    • Hydrogen bonds: Green dashed lines running horizontally between adjacent strands, connecting the pleated ridges. Show 5-6 green dashed lines between each pair of adjacent strands.
    • R-group stubs: Small colored bumps projecting above and below the plane of the sheet, alternating up/down along each strand. About 3-4 per strand.

    Visual feel: Two clearly different secondary structures — one is a spiral coil, the other is a flat pleated arrangement. Both are stabilized by the same green hydrogen bonds.

    Panel 3 — Tertiary Structure

    Draw a compact, globular folded protein filling most of the panel. This shows the entire polypeptide chain folded into its 3D shape.

    • Overall shape: An irregular, roughly spherical/globular mass — NOT a perfect sphere, but a lumpy, organic shape like a kidney or potato
    • Secondary structure elements visible inside:
    • 2-3 orange-red alpha helices (shown as coiled ribbon segments) embedded within the globular shape
    • 1-2 blue beta sheet regions (shown as flat arrow clusters) visible within the mass
    • Gray loops and coils connecting the helices and sheets
    • Interior (hydrophobic core): The center of the protein should be slightly darker/denser, showing that nonpolar amino acids (gray circles) cluster together away from water. Show 4-5 small gray circles packed in the interior.
    • Surface (hydrophilic exterior): The outside of the protein should show polar (blue) and charged (red, orange) amino acid bumps exposed to the water environment.
    • Disulfide bridges: 1-2 gold/yellow lines crossing through the protein interior, connecting two points on the surface. These should look like thick gold staples or bridges holding distant parts of the chain together.
    • Salt bridge: One pair of a red circle and an orange circle close together on the surface, with a small lightning-bolt or spark shape between them (no text — just a visual indicator of electrostatic attraction)
    • Hydrogen bonds between R-groups: 2-3 small green dashed lines visible on the surface between nearby amino acid bumps

    Visual feel: A complex, folded 3D structure with visible elements of secondary structure inside. The emphasis is on COMPACT FOLDING with multiple types of stabilizing interactions.

    Panel 4 — Quaternary Structure (rightmost)

    Draw a hemoglobin tetramer — four protein subunits assembled together into a functional complex.

    • Overall arrangement: Four globular subunits packed together symmetrically, like four lumpy balls nestled together in a 2×2 arrangement
    • Alpha subunits (×2): Light blue (#AED6F1) globular masses, positioned at upper-left and lower-right of the tetramer. Each should show faint coiled ribbon segments (alpha helices) within, similar to the tertiary panel but smaller and lighter.
    • Beta subunits (×2): Light pink/salmon (#F5B7B1) globular masses, positioned at upper-right and lower-left. Same internal structure hints as the alpha subunits.
    • Subunit interfaces: Where the four subunits touch, there should be visible contact surfaces — slight flattening where they press together, with a slightly darker line at the interface showing the boundary between subunits.
    • Heme groups: Inside each of the four subunits, one flat disk shape in dark red/maroon (#922B21). The disk should be oriented at a slight angle so it is clearly visible as a flat plate embedded within the globular subunit. Each disk is roughly 20% the diameter of its host subunit.
    • Oxygen molecules: 2-3 small bright red spheres (much smaller than the heme disks) sitting on top of or adjacent to the heme disks, representing O₂ binding. Not all four heme groups need to have oxygen bound — showing 2 with O₂ and 2 without suggests cooperative binding.

    Visual feel: Multiple individual protein subunits (each resembling the tertiary structure) assembled into a larger functional machine. The emphasis is on MULTIPLE SUBUNITS WORKING TOGETHER.

    Composition Notes

    • All four panels should be the same width
    • The structures should increase in visual complexity from left to right (simple linear chain → complex multi-subunit assembly)
    • Each structure should be centered vertically and horizontally within its panel
    • Leave enough white space around each structure for overlay labels (at least 10% padding on each side)
    • Color fills must be flat or with very subtle shading — no heavy gradients, no photorealistic rendering, no drop shadows
    • The style should be consistent across all four panels — same line weights, same level of detail, same artistic approach
    • Each panel's content should be roughly the same visual "weight" — don't make the primary structure tiny and the quaternary structure huge

    No text, labels, numbers, arrows, callout lines, leader lines, or annotation marks anywhere in the image. This is critical — the interactive overlay adds all labels programmatically.
